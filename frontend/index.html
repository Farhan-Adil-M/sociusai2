How it works

  1. 1

    Add your material

    Upload a PDF, slides, a Word file, or paste a link to a web page. Socius AI reads the text and keeps it in your own library.

  2. 2

    It finds the right passage

    Before it answers anything, it searches your material and quotes the passage it used — with the file name and the page number.

  3. 3

    Practise at your level

    Quizzes start easy and move up as you get topics right. Miss something and the next set eases back down.

  4. 4

    Review with flashcards

    The same material becomes flashcards with a review schedule, so you spend your time on what you are about to forget.

What you get

Answers with receipts

Every answer points at the file and page it came from. The page numbers come from what was stored when you uploaded, never from the AI's memory.

Marking you can check

Your score is worked out by a fixed rule over the answers you gave. The AI writes the questions and explanations; it never decides your mark.

Progress that stays honest

A topic you have not been tested on says not assessed yet. It is never quietly rounded up to a full score, and your history survives restarts.

It says when it does not know

With nothing in your library to ground an answer, the tutor tells you so and suggests a topic from your own material instead of guessing.

Where this is today

Socius AI is a working prototype built by a small team. It is honest about what it can and cannot do.

  • Answers are AI-written. They can be wrong. Check them against the page each citation points to.
  • Scoring and levels are not. They come from simple, written-down rules, so the same answers always give the same result.
  • Your original files are not kept. Only the text is stored, so you can rebuild the search index without uploading again.
  • Legacy .doc files are not supported. PDFs, PowerPoint, Word (.docx) and web pages are.

Start with one file.

You do not need to move your whole course across. Add one lecture, ask one question, and see whether the answer matches the page it cites.
